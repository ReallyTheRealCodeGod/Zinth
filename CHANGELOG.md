# Changelog

Newest first. Each entry is one roadmap item, finished, built, and verified with the theory check.

## 2026-09-10

- Section fades. Beside Sweep in the inspector every section now has a **Fade** choice: none, ◢ in or ◣ out. In lifts the whole mix out of silence over the section's first two bars, the way a song starts; out lets it fall away to silence over its last two, the way a song ends. A section shorter than two bars fades over its whole length. It is a master gain sitting after the limiter — the very last thing in the chain — so a fade carries everything, layers and drums and the delay and reverb returns and the risers, and nothing downstream fights it; the scope and the level meter read after it too, so you see a fade as well as hear it. Outros come set to fade out, so a fresh track already ends gracefully, and any section can fade: the one after a fade-out eases back to full level, and stopping playback always leaves the mix wide open, so the play-along keys are never left silent. The shape lives in `Z.fadePlan(mode,steps,stepSec)` with a knee partway down — a single exponential ramp would spend half a fade below hearing, so most of the two bars is spent between full level and −20 dB, the way a hand on a fader moves. The scheduler, the offline render in `exportWav` and the MIDI export all read that one plan: a WAV fades exactly like what you heard, and a fading section reaches your DAW as CC7 volume automation, sampled a beat at a time down the same curve. A faded section shows ◢ or ◣ beside its layer dots in the arrangement, the choice is part of each section in the project snapshot so it autosaves, undoes and survives Save and Open, and the theory check proves a fade only ever moves one way, stays inside its own section, never reaches a gain of nought, spends its middle in the range you can actually hear and always hands the next section a full mix.
- Per-section filter sweeps. Every section now has a **Sweep** choice in the inspector: none, ↗ up or ↘ down. Up starts the whole mix dark at 300 Hz and opens it over the section's own length — the classic way into a song or up into a drop — and down plays open and closes over the last bar, for an outro or a hand-off into the next part. It is a master low-pass sitting between the mix and the punch-in chain, so it carries everything: layers, drums, the delay and reverb returns, risers and crashes. Intros, pre-choruses and breaks come set to open up and outros to close down, so a fresh track already breathes; any section can be set to none. A swept section shows ↗ or ↘ beside its layer dots in the arrangement, the sweep repeats each pass while you loop a section, and stopping playback always leaves the mix wide open so the play-along keys never sound muffled. The shape lives in `Z.sweepPlan(mode,steps,stepSec)` and both the scheduler and the offline render in `exportWav` schedule it from there, so a WAV sweeps exactly like what you heard. The choice is part of each section in the project snapshot, so it autosaves, undoes, and survives Save and Open, and the theory check proves a plan starts and ends on an audible frequency, never runs past its own section and always hands the next one a wide-open mix.
- Chord editing on the chord cards. The cards above the arrangement are the progression now: click one and a chord editor opens under it. Pick another degree of the key, give that one chord its seventh whatever the track's 7ths setting says, choose which chord tone sits in the bass — auto leaves it to the voice leading — and set how long it lasts, 1, 2 or 4 bars, so a part can be I for four bars then IV for two and V for two. Split cuts a chord in two to make room for a new one, Remove takes one away, and ↺ Generated brings the rolled progression back. The progression always fills the eight bars of the loop: what no longer fits steps aside, and the last chord stretches to cover the rest, so the arp, the bass and the melody always have a chord under them. An inverted chord reads as a slash chord on its card. Entries live in `state.prog[part]` as `{d,bars,seventh,inv}` objects beside the plain degree arrays a chord-box sketch produces, so both forms play, autosave, undo, survive Save and Open, and reach the WAV and MIDI exports; a track code carries them as `0x4-3x2-4x27i1` and old codes still load. The cards are keyboard operable, <kbd>←</kbd> and <kbd>→</kbd> step between them while the editor is open and <kbd>Esc</kbd> closes it, and the theory check proves an edited progression keeps its degrees, bar lengths, sevenths and inversions, covers exactly eight bars and stays inside the chord scale in every key and scale.
- Record the bass and the arp from the keys. A "Record into" segment sits beside Rec — lead, arp or bass — and <kbd>I</kbd> cycles it. While Rec is armed the letter keys sound through the layer you are recording into and land where that layer sings: the lead at the pitch you press, the arp an octave up inside its own roll lane, the bass folded into MIDI 36–59. The pitch class never changes, so a take is as locked to the key as a drawn note. The generated layer steps aside while you play over it, whichever layer it is, and the take is stored in `state.arpEdits[part]` or `state.bassEdits[part]` beside the notes you draw — so recording and drawing remain one path that autosaves, undoes, survives Save and Open, and reaches the WAV and MIDI exports. Clear melody follows the choice and becomes Clear arp or Clear bass, the keys press in the layer's colour while armed, and the status line says if the section or the mixer would keep your take silent. `Z.recordPitch` carries the rule, and the theory check runs it over every scale in all 12 keys.
- Note editing for the arp and bass lanes. Both lanes are now a row per pitch of the key, like the lead lane: click an empty spot to add a note, click a note to remove it, drag its right edge to change its length. The arp draws from the chord scale two octaves up, the bass from the chord scale inside its own register, and a drawn bass note that the section's key shift pushes out of MIDI 36–59 folds back by octaves, so the bass never strays out of its register. Edits live in `state.arpEdits[part]` and `state.bassEdits[part]`, the same format a recorded melody uses, and `generateTrack` honours them through `arpEvents` and `bassEvents` exactly as it does `leadEvents` — so they autosave, undo, redo, survive Save and Open, and reach the WAV and MIDI exports. A ↺ badge appears beside a lane's name once its notes are yours and brings the generated ones back. The roll is 420 px tall now so the pitch rows are easier to hit, and the theory check proves drawn arp and bass notes come back verbatim, follow the section key shift, stay in the chord scale and keep the bass in register.
- Piano-roll note editing for the lead. The lead lane of the roll is now a row per scale pitch: click an empty spot to add a note, click a note to remove it, drag its right edge to change its length with a live preview. The lead stays one line, so a note already sounding is trimmed rather than buried. Edits land in `state.leadEdits[part]`, the same store a recorded melody uses, so they autosave, undo, redo, survive Save and Open, and reach the WAV and MIDI exports; the first edit freezes the generated melody and Clear melody brings it back. The theory check now proves that edited and recorded notes come back verbatim, follow the section's key shift and stay in scale.
- Recording melodies from the keys, undo and redo, transitions, MIDI export, first-run guide and shortcut sheet, oscilloscope, theory self-test, sources split into `src/`.
- Chord builder fix: whole-tone seventh chords no longer double the root (the six-note scale wraps the seventh onto the root; the sixth is used instead). Found by the theory check.
